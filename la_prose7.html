Excerpt from my Novel in Progress

  • By Lyla Levitt
  • Prose / Novel Excerpts

Lyla Levitt is a rising junior at Windermere Preparatory School in Florida. She has just completed publishing her school's first literary journal edition on behalf of her student body, and has been published in her school's online newspaper on multiple occasions. She was born and raised in Westchester, New York, and has been a competitive swimmer for eleven years. In addition to swimming, she enjoys compound and recurve archery.

This is an excerpt from my novel in progress. It is inspired by views from New York, people watching.

I need you to remember I’m dead. There is no other way for me to say it. I’m not upset, I'm angry. Actually, I’d like to take that back. I am not angry. I am filled with rage. I am left as a rotting carcass. The only emotion I took with me was rage. The sole feeling I had towards others was resentment. What a terrible thing.

It was not a difficult case to understand. It was written in my diary, on walls, on my mirror in lipstick. Maybe it did make me look crazy, like I needed a different kind of help. But I swear on everything I love that is still living that I was not losing my mind, nor did I have an imagination that was “too realistic”. I told you he was coming for me; I swore on my life I—

I'm dead, remember?

I was going to tell you, that I swear on my life, I did not go crazy. I admit that maybe in those last few minutes I did lose it a bit; I felt like I was stuck in a perpetual circle of time. Lying on the cold concrete floor, I heard the same movie running for a week, and then just as I had arrived, I was gone.

You see, I have—no, I had a friend Michael Sterling, whose father owned the movie theatre one street over from my house. I grew up in a small town. The kind of place where everyone knew everyone and gossip spread rapidly and with ease. So, as soon as my own mother believed I was clinically insane, the whole town was in agreement within three short days.

The following week was hell. All 2,381 people in our town thought I was absolutely batshit. Actually, make that 2,379 people in town, to account for myself and Michael.

My small town had a small schooling system that offered enough of an education to go to a nice college, and maybe a future career. However, it did always terrify me how many people chose to stay in town after school. Honestly, now that I’m thinking about it, I don’t know anybody who left. The thought of marrying and having a family with somebody I've known my whole life, never meeting new people, and never truly amounting to anything was very unappealing to me.

But for a town full of people who were mostly from the area or neighboring towns, they were very frightened by the thought of death.

I will never understand people being extremely terrified of “the end” or death or whatever you classify as the limit to which your existence can stretch. We are born from stardust, and we will eventually end as the same. But how can all of these people be so fearful of dying yet have no motivation or drive to get out of the box that confines them and actually become something?

It feels like we're all… trapped?

Anyway, in my opinion, you should be trying to achieve a life you love, not spending every waking second being worried that your brief time on this earth is going to end. There is a Latin sentiment that reminds me to make the most of the time I have, but to not fear death in itself. Memento mori, ergo, carpe diem. Everybody has their own expiration date, why try to outrun it?

Bearing in mind the goal to create a life you love, you also need people you love. Human life was created on the basis of human connection. Knowing this, I realized I needed friends, and I figured I may find some in school.

I only met Michael in the 3rd grade; he was not a new transfer to the school, I was just “anti-social”. Come to think of it, nobody has ever transferred into my school district, but I suppose that’s just how small towns are. Aside from that, Michael and I were best friends within a week.

To be honest, the requirements to obtain “best friend” status in the third grade were relatively minuscule, seeing as there was not much to keep secret or protect each other from. I mean, we were eight years old; what do you expect?

So, as any kid does, I ran home to my parents to tell them that I finally made a friend, but it did not exactly have the desired outcome. That’s not to say my parents were upset or disappointed with me, they just did not have much of a reaction at all because it was expected of children to socialize and make friends with their classmates.

However, I was left with the unsettling feeling of mediocrity, which set the tone for the rest of my life. Unlike the remnants of my antisocial childhood, my roots of having perspectives estranged from others whilst remaining terribly average in the social skills department are very much still daily battles.

With my permanent mediocrity in mind, I decided I would do absolutely anything to keep Michael in my life. I truly thought he was the only friend I would ever have that was an actual friend. Not only somebody that cares about you, but somebody who is willing to talk the light back into your life, and when you do not want the light, they will sit with you in the dark.

I believe that everybody deserves to have that person, their very own Michael. I was ecstatic to find mine. I did as I needed to for the last 8 years of my life, just as I said I would, anything to keep Michael as that same best friend. Thank goodness I did because I was also right about the fact that I would never make any more friends, not a single one.

Michael grew up 6 houses down from me on Sunrise Avenue, and from the second we met, we made traditions. First came the weekly bike rides. Every Sunday we would meet up at his house; his mother would make us a brunch of pancakes, waffles, french toast, and so much more.

Then we’d set out on our bike ride to debrief our week. The trail was a 10-mile path on thick black pavement that had been poured over what was once a railroad. Surrounded by forests full of tall green blocking the sun's light, it was always the perfect temperature for a ride. It was our secret spot to talk about anything and everything on our minds.

I’m not really sure if anyone else knew about the place, nobody was ever there aside from Michael and myself; but, we liked it that way, without a singular rule or restriction, only freedom.
