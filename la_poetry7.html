Selected Poems by Myka Steggall

  • By Myka Steggall
  • Poetry

Myka Steggall is a rising junior at Oberlin High School in Oberlin, OH, and she has been writing her entire life. Her most recent accolade came from the Maltz Museum of Jewish Heritage, from which she won first place in their Stop the Hate Poetry Contest. Her main forms of writing include poetry, personal essays, and novels/novellas. Apart from writing, she enjoys playing the cello and the double bass, participating in theater in her school and community, watching and studying films, sketching fashion designs, and reading whatever she can get her hands on. She plans on majoring in creative writing, becoming a famous author, and disappearing to be a hermit in the Irish countryside.

A Seamster and an Author Visit the Brooklyn Promenade

Those shades of blue are the stuff of dreams, huh?
The rivers below, the sky above, the glittering glass between.
What I’d give for a bolt of that.
If only I could put the difference into words. ‘Light’ doesn’t cut it.
What do you reckon it’s made of? Silk? Chiffon?
…Something gentle and mellisonant. It’s quiet up here.
There wouldn’t be another dress like it in the world.
They’d give me a Pulitzer for figuring it out, for sure.
It would move like the waves with every step, with that frothy white hem the boats kick up behind them.
Then again, there are a million books about New York. She really is a muse.
Wash delicately with cold water and ambition.
I can see why.

Homecoming

Poor pretty girl
with the deer-in-headlights eyes
Drifts around parties wearing a human disguise
She dries her own tears
and breaks her own bones
Crying over getting used to being on her own
Begs for a chair
At the emptiest tables
The princess in a tower from her childhood fables
Barbed wire words
She doesn’t mean to say
Nobody listens when she kneels on scratched knees to pray
She went to homecoming alone
Smile fake as the plastic mirrorball
Such a pretty dress just to stand against the wall
She goes home early but keeps herself dolled up
Waltzes alone in her room until the early morning light
Only in good company in her dreams at night

Purgatory Is a Subway Platform

stench of rotten metal
from within the rust catacombs
like cliffs succumb to the ocean
chunks break off from the edge of the stone
it’s suffocatingly hot and thick
the air soaks ghostly limbs in cold sweat
trains arrive above, below, and all around
but headlights will never appear in your tunnels
you wonder, sometimes, who is coming and going
if you arrived on a subway you can’t remember
the broken screen promises 10 more minutes
of eternal waiting

Angel at the Whitney

Inspired by Jay DeFeo’s The Rose

I turned the corner of the gallery
to see an angel framed against the wall.
They say they had to dismantle the studio window
and use a forklift to get it out.
You must feel strange, I said,
shielding my eyes,
to be in a museum instead of a chapel.
Not necessarily, the angel replied.
People desperate enough can turn anywhere
into a chapel.
Their rooftop, a hospital waiting room,
the bathroom at a party.
Besides, you’ve knelt before me to write.
That is worship enough, no?
